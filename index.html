Creative Strategist Breakthrough — Sean Ferres

The whole business is one page, one form, one call. A 9-minute VSL at go.seanferres.com, a 60-second Typeform, a Calendly booking. There is no opt-in, no webinar and no email capture anywhere in the path — if you do not apply, he has nothing to follow up with. Behind it sits an ad account of 360 lifetime records where 250 of them are the same piece of copy. The thing worth stealing is not the funnel. It is the lead: a dated news event (Meta's Andromeda update) that manufactures urgency off the calendar instead of off a fake timer.

What matters

The lead is a dated news event, and that is the one thing worth stealing

He does not open on a promise or a pain. He opens on a calendar fact: Zuck burned $80bn on the metaverse, redirected the compute into rebuilding the ad engine, the result is called Andromeda, and it needs volume rather than targeting. Therefore brands that ran five ads now need 25–50 per campaign. Therefore a new role exists and the window to claim it is closing. Urgency comes off the event, not off a countdown timer. Nothing on the page is scarce; the market is.

One concept is 69% of everything he has ever run

Of 360 lifetime ad records, 250 carry the same body copy — “Big clients use your low price as a filter… to screen you OUT.” It ran January to May 2026 and 30 are still live. There are only 24 distinct bodies in the entire history of the account. He does not write new angles; he re-uploads the winner and lets Andromeda sort out delivery. He is running the playbook he sells.

July 2026 added a second layer, and none of it is bundled

Nine new ads went live in July and August, every one a single record with its own copy: Sam $15k→$130k in a month, AJ $10k→$100k in seven months, Akhil $25k/mo in 60 days, Jack Woods, a sunset walk with Matt Volkwyn, and a Hormozi-adjacent authority play. All nine are still live. Read against the ladder these are solution- and product-aware assets sitting on top of one unaware-stage workhorse.

There is no email capture anywhere before the call

No opt-in page, no lead magnet in the paid path, no newsletter, no phone field. The captured pages return an empty forms[] array. If a visitor does not fill the Typeform, he has nothing to follow up with and has to re-buy that person with retargeting. Both Shelby and Brook capture the email first. This is the biggest structural weakness in the funnel and it is the one thing not to copy.

The Typeform title leaks his naming convention

The application's page title is CS Breakthrough App Master Funnel v1 - Talking Head. He version-numbers his funnels and tags them by creative format. “Talking Head” is the format this whole master funnel was built around, which lines up with 262 of 360 records being video. Same class of leak as CreatorHive naming its split test in the <title> tag.

The price is four figures, and the refusal to name it is the tactic

There is a 2m48s video called “FAQ 5 — What’s the Investment?” sitting on the confirmed page. It says: “This is a one time, four figure investment, not an impulse buy… most students invest a few thousand dollars.” Then he explains why he will not give one number: it depends on 1:1 versus group and on payment plan, and — the real move — “we do raise the price every so often… the price only moves in one direction and that is up. So whatever the investment is now will be the lowest it’ll ever be.” He converts the absence of a price into a scarcity argument. Software is broken out separately at ~$50/mo, with an optional “AI outreach machine” at a couple hundred more.

The confirmed page is a 48-minute objection library and it is the best asset here

Fourteen Wistia videos, 48m06s total, one per objection: what is Reverse Outreach, what does a creative strategist actually do, will this work if I’m a beginner, how much time, how long until I make money, how is this different, I’m not good with tech, what countries, why now, and what’s the investment. Plus a 9m55s pre-call video, a client logo wall (Hormozi, Robbins, Bet-David, Graziosi, Brunson, Kennedy, Martell, Belfort) and named testimonials. This is the Brook Hiddink pre-call-workshop pattern, and it is the single most copyable thing in the funnel.

His own file names date the repositioning to mid-May 2026

Wistia's JSON-LD tags every video with the funnel that commissioned it. Nine are HAMMER THEM FUNNEL 2.0 - COPYWRITERS 2026, uploaded 23 Feb. Five are HAMMER THEM FUNNEL 3.0 - CS BREAKTHROUGH, uploaded 14–16 May. That is the same month as the ad cohort that is still 100% alive. The pivot, the new creative and the surviving ads are one event, and we can date it to the week.

The confirmed page still runs the OLD videos, and they contradict the new pitch

CAUTION Nine of the fourteen are 2.0-era. The investment FAQ a freshly-booked lead watches still says the goal is “$10,000 a month as a copywriter” and calls the program the “CMB breakthrough challenge”. Ben’s Calendly page sells “$5k–10k/mo as an AI-powered copywriter… Reverse Outreach + AI”. The ad and the VSL sell “creative strategist”. The Andromeda repositioning is a front-end layer over a product that has not finished moving. A prospect who watches everything gets three different offer names in one funnel.

Applying enrols you in a live training that appears nowhere else

UNVERIFIED The DQ ending reads: “the Creative Strategist 101 free live training you just signed up for”. So the application silently registers you for a training that is not mentioned on the VSL page, in any ad, or on any captured step. We could not locate a page for it. If it is real, the “no webinar” read on this funnel is incomplete.

The confirmed page throws away everything Calendly hands it

Calendly redirects with assigned_to, event_type_name, event_start_time, invitee_full_name and text_reminder_number in the query string. The page reads none of them — no URLSearchParams, no personalisation, no name, no rendered appointment time. For a four-figure offer that is a free trust beat left on the floor.

He rebuilt the offer, not just the creative

Everything from 2025 is switched off. The Copy MBA page (108248635378423) is running zero ads. The AI-copywriting positioning — “Crypto's window closed. AI is the next gold rush”, the robot-chef ad, the Bigfoot AI-video ad, the freecopyclass.com live webinar and the copymba.com VSL — is 100% dead across ~100 records and 13 concepts. He moved the ads to a plain-name page (“Sean Ferres”, 438598856674850) and changed mechanism from webinar to VSL-plus-application.

The guarantee does the work the price cannot

With no price on the page, the risk-reversal has to carry the whole close: “we'll keep working with you for free until you make at least $5,000 in new income”. It is scoped to a number, not a refund, and it is conditional on implementation. Cheaper for him than a money-back guarantee and it reads stronger.

He names his own selfish motive on purpose

A full 45 seconds of a 9-minute VSL is spent explaining that he runs the program to train people he wants to hire or refer, and that good graduates get paid to write for his campaigns and for his network. It converts the offer from “buy my course” into “join my hiring pipeline”, and it pre-empts the “why are you teaching this instead of doing it” objection before it forms.

Proof is a ladder, deliberately, with the top rung disclaimed

He gives three tiers and labels them: Sam at $49k from one client then $78k the next month (extreme), AJ at $1k → $10k/mo (“here's what typical looks like”), and Charney at one $2k/mo retainer (“on the other end of the spectrum”). He then explicitly says “I'm absolutely not saying you're gonna come in here and start making seven figures”. The disclaimer buys credibility for the mid-tier number, which is the one he actually wants believed.

The whole application, and its gate, are published in the page source

Typeform ships the entire form model in the HTML. Form AhJWFsEs, 9 fields, 3 endings, nothing submitted. The questions: experience level, 90-day goal, hours per week, current monthly income, contact info, why now (long text), what you could invest in the next 14 days (under $500 / $500–2k / $2k–4k / $4k+), phone number, then the Calendly block. The DQ rule is a single OR: hours = under 7/week, OR budget = under $500, OR experience = “what’s copywriting?”. Everything else is always → Pre-Approved. Income is collected but does not gate — you can earn under $3k/mo and still reach the call. The phone number is captured at Q8, before the DQ fires.

The DQ path is a downsell, not a door

Fail and you do not get a dead end. You get: “CMB isn’t the right fit yet. Our coaching is designed for ambitious copywriters who can invest at least 10 hours a week and a few thousand dollars…” plus a YouTube crash course and a push into the free live training. The rejection copy is also the only place the price appears before booking — he tells you what it costs at the exact moment he tells you that you cannot have it.
