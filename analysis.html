Analysis

What they do, why it works, and what is worth taking.

The one-line read. Ferres runs the simplest funnel in this entire swipe file — ad, VSL, form, call — and spends all his leverage on the lead instead. The Andromeda news event does the work a webinar, an opt-in sequence and a countdown timer would normally have to do. That is a trade worth understanding before copying either half of it.

The news-event lead, taken apart

This is the structure, stripped of his specifics. Every step is a fact the reader can check, which is what stops it reading as hype:

BeatWhat it doesHis version
1. The eventAn external, dated, verifiable thing happened Meta rebuilt its ad engine. It is called Andromeda.
2. The mechanismWhy the event changes the economics It no longer needs manual targeting. It needs volume.
3. The consequenceA concrete, countable new demand Brands that ran 5 ads now need 25–50 creatives per campaign.
4. The vacancyWho the market must now pay, and what they are called Nobody in-house can do it. The role is “creative strategist”.
5. The closing windowUrgency off the calendar, not off a timer “Over the next 30–90 days a wave of brands all pick their person and settle in for 12+ months.”

Step 5 is the one most people get wrong. He never says “this offer closes Friday”. He says the market closes, and puts a plausible horizon on it. Nothing to disprove, nothing to expire, and it survives the ad running for eight straight months.

Where this points harder at us than at him. The same event is a stronger argument for us. Fifty videos a month is a filming problem before it is a writing problem — a marketing department cannot produce that volume in-house at any price, and a studio is too slow. His version needs the reader to accept that copywriting is being repriced. Ours only needs them to accept that someone has to hold the camera. That work is already drafted in the Copy SSOT inbox as the Andromeda news-event ad.

What to take, and what to leave

 ElementWhy
TAKEThe news-event lead structure Urgency that does not decay and cannot be disproved. Already drafted for us.
TAKENaming the selfish reason out loud Converts “buy my course” into “join my pipeline” and kills the why-are-you-teaching-this objection before it forms. We have a stronger version of this claim than he does, because the placements are real.
TAKEThe disclaimed proof ladder Extreme / typical / floor, with the extreme explicitly disclaimed. Buys belief for the middle number, which is the one that sells.
TAKEScoped guarantee to a dollar figure “Free until you make $5,000” is cheaper than a refund and reads stronger.
WATCHOne concept, 250 uploads Correct for Andromeda, but it means a single point of failure. When that concept fatigues he has 23 other bodies in eight months of history to fall back on.
LEAVENo email capture before the call Every non-applicant is lost and must be re-bought.
TAKEThe 48-minute objection library on the confirmed page One video per objection, watched between booking and the call. Same pattern as Brook's pre-call workshop. This is the highest-leverage thing on this site and we do not have an equivalent.
TAKE“The price only moves up, so today is the lowest it will ever be” Turns refusing to name a price into a reason to act now. Costs nothing and it is true of most programs.
LEAVEThree offer names in one funnel Ad says creative strategist, Calendly says AI-powered copywriter, confirmed page says Copy Millions Blueprint, and the investment video says “$10k/mo as a copywriter”. A prospect who watches everything gets four different products. Pick one name and hold it.
LEAVEThe unsourced volume claim “25–50 creatives per campaign” is his number, repeated without a source. If we run the same event we source it or soften it to “dozens”.

Worth internalising as a habit: check the title tag and the video schema on every funnel you swipe. CreatorHive named its split test in the <title>; Ferres named his whole roadmap in his video metadata. Neither cost anything to find.
